**HA Power Bill AU** calculates Australian electricity costs and solar export credits
from your Home Assistant energy meters. Set up your electricity contract in the UI
to price your energy use in Australian dollars (AUD).
Rate changes, time-of-use windows, feed-in tariffs, daily charges, demand charges and GST are all
handled, and history is rebuilt from your recorder statistics.

The integration is named **HA Power Bill AU** in Home Assistant and uses the domain `ha_power_bill_au`.
Source code and issues are hosted at
[`cloudbr34k84/ha-power-bill-au`](https://github.com/cloudbr34k84/ha-power-bill-au).

## What it creates

For each contract:

- Current tariff (the active import band) and the current import rate (GST-inclusive)
- Import cost today and month to date (daily charges included), and net cost
- Export credit today and month to date, when an export meter is set
- kWh today and kWh month to date for each import and export band (for example peak, shoulder,
  off-peak), which reset each day and month like a utility meter, with no selects or automations
- Lifetime totals for import cost, export credit and each tracked meter. These use the
  `total` state class, so you can point the Energy dashboard at them.

## Installation

Requires Home Assistant **2025.8.0 or later**, Recorder enabled and cumulative energy sensors
measured in Wh, kWh or MWh. Readings are normalized to kWh. Costs currently use AUD and
Australian GST rules.

1. HACS → Integrations → three-dot menu → Custom repositories
2. Add `https://github.com/cloudbr34k84/ha-power-bill-au`, category **Integration**
3. Install **HA Power Bill AU** and restart Home Assistant

4. Open **Settings → Devices & services → Add integration → HA Power Bill AU**.

## Configuration

1. Settings → Devices & Services → Add integration → **HA Power Bill AU**
2. Name the contract, then choose the import meter (and the export meter, if you have one).
   Both must be energy sensors with state class `total` or `total_increasing` and a unit
   of Wh, kWh or MWh. Import, export and tracked-device meters must be distinct.
3. Enter the import bands: up to three daily bands, each with a start hour, end hour and rate.
   Bands must cover all 24 hours exactly once. A band can wrap past midnight (start 21, end 11).
4. If you have an export meter, enter the feed-in bands the same way.
5. Enter whether the rates include GST, the daily supply and membership charges, and any
   extra meters to track (for example an EV charger).

### Changing rates later

Settings → Devices & Services → HA Power Bill AU → Configure:

- **Add a plan from a date** for a rate or plan change. Usage from that date is priced with the
  new plan. Earlier usage keeps the plan it was recorded under.
- **Demand charge** if your plan charges for your highest kW each month.
- **Stepped feed-in** if your solar feed-in tariff pays a higher rate for the first kWh each day.
- **Tracked devices** to add or remove extra energy sensors (an EV charger, a pool pump). Each
  one gets cost today, month to date and lifetime sensors. Deselecting a device removes its sensors.

Changes take effect straight away; the integration reloads itself.

### Reading your bill

**Demand charge.** On the bill it is the line `Demand (KW/Day)`:

```
Demand (KW/Day)  01/11/2024 - 30/11/2024  14.30 KW  $0.1850  $79.37
```

- `$0.1850` is the **demand rate** (per kW per day).
- `14.30 KW` is the **billed kW** for that month. Enter it as `2024-11: 14.3`, one month per line.
- The charge is rate × kW × days in the month (0.185 × 14.30 × 30 = $79.37).

If your bill has no demand line, leave this alone.

**Estimating demand from your meter.** You don't have to wait for the bill. Switch on
*Estimate demand from my meter* and HA Power Bill AU works out your highest demand itself, so the
month's demand charge builds up in your costs as the month goes on.

- Set the **window** and **block** to match the bill's wording. For
  `Demand 7 days a week: 4:00pm to 9:00pm` use 16 to 21. Most retailers average over 30 minutes.
- Your demand is the highest average kW over any one block that starts on the clock (4:00, 4:30,
  and so on) inside the window. Blocks are not slid, because the retailer's meter doesn't slide them.
- It reads the import meter's 5-minute statistics, which Home Assistant keeps for about a week
  (the recorder's `purge_keep_days`). The highest block so far each month is saved, so it
  survives restarts. Switch the estimate on early in a month.
- A kW you enter for a month always replaces the estimate. The **Peak demand this month** sensor
  shows the figure being charged, and its attributes show the estimate next to the billed kW.
- Treat the demand figure as an estimate. Meter sampling and retailer rules can differ;
  enter the billed kW when available to reconcile it.

**Stepped feed-in.** On the bill these are `Solar FiT - Step 1` and `Solar FiT - Step 2` lines,
at two different rates. Step 1 usually covers the first few kWh you export each day. Enter the
two rates and the number of kWh per day that step 1 covers. If your bill has one feed-in rate
(or time-of-use feed-in bands), you don't need this.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Some hours of the day are not covered"**: a band is missing or ends too early. Check the end hours.
- **Costs are unavailable**: check that the meter reports a finite, nonnegative value in
  Wh, kWh or MWh, with energy device class and state class `total` or `total_increasing`.
  Tariff information remains available while the meter is unavailable.
- **History looks wrong**: check the meter's hourly statistics in Developer tools → Statistics.
  HA Power Bill AU rebuilds history from them on every reload.
- **Support information**: download diagnostics from the HA Power Bill AU integration entry.
  Diagnostics include configuration counts and meter availability, without provider names,
  meter entity IDs, tariff rates or usage amounts.

## Limitations

- One import and one export meter per contract.
- Hourly bands only. Demand is estimated from the meter (optional) or entered per month from your bill.
- Stepped feed-in is modelled as a daily cap (step 1 for the first kWh of each day).
- Historical fixed charges start on the first available import-meter statistics date.
  Without recorder history, they start on the first setup day; that date and locally measured
  usage are preserved across reloads. Older versions used installation-specific dates, so
  rebuilding with this version can correct previously overstated lifetime fixed charges.
- Hourly statistics take priority over daily totals for the same day. Daily-only history
  is an approximation priced at the plan's noon rate; it cannot recover time-of-use detail.
- Residential spike filters ignore statistics above 30 kWh per hour, 300 kWh per day and
  4 kWh per five-minute demand slot. These limits may exclude valid high-capacity usage.
- History before the first dated plan uses that plan's rates.

## Removal

Settings → Devices & Services → HA Power Bill AU → Delete. Removing a contract deletes its stored
usage and demand data. Remove the integration files through
HACS, or delete `custom_components/ha_power_bill_au` after removing all its entries, then restart
Home Assistant. Do not manually edit Home Assistant's `.storage` files.

## Recovery

Back up Home Assistant before upgrading or changing historical plans. To recover from a failed
upgrade, reinstall the previous release and restart. Restore your Home Assistant backup if you
also need to recover previous settings or stored data.
